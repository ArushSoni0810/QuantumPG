The Science

Quantum Superposition
as an Entropy Source

In classical computing, random number generators like Python's random.randint() are pseudo-random — deterministic algorithms seeded by system time or OS state. Given the seed, the output is entirely predictable.

Quantum mechanics operates on an entirely different principle. A qubit in superposition simultaneously exists as 0 and 1 with certain probabilities. When measured, it collapses to a definite state — and this collapse is fundamentally, provably random. No hidden variable can predict it.

QuantumKey simulates this process by modeling quantum probability amplitudes and sampling from them using the Born rule — the same mathematics that governs real quantum hardware like IBM's and Google's quantum processors.

The result: passwords that are cryptographically unpredictable, not just statistically random.

Why Not Just Use randint()?

Classical RNG (Python randint)

Mersenne Twister algorithm — deterministic, seeded, reproducible under analysis. Fine for simulations, not for secrets.

  • Pseudo-random, not truly random
  • Predictable with enough observations
  • Seed leakage = full compromise
  • No physical entropy source
  • Fast, deterministic
  • Reproducible (useful for games)
